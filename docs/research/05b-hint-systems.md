# Research 5b — Help for stuck players: earned, diegetic, non-spoiling

Source: research subagent, 2026-09-04 02:10 KST. Problem: playtester had every card in hand and no in-game way to get unstuck; owner rejects a hint ladder that "feels like giving the answer". Each entry: mechanism · reveals vs withholds · trigger/pacing · what we borrow.

## The pattern across all of them
Acclaimed systems separate three kinds of help and rarely ship the third: **where** (an incomplete location/thread — Outer Wilds, Hitman), **which** (the piece that matters, not its meaning — Ace Attorney, Golden Idol, Portal 2), **what** (the deduction — only priced or on repeated explicit request: Layton, MI2 SE, UHS). A fourth channel isn't a hint at all: **partial-correctness feedback** on an attempt (Obra Dinn, Golden Idol) — the thing that actually says "you're on the right track".

## Case studies

- **Outer Wilds — ship log, rumor mode, "There's more to explore here".** Mechanism: a detective corkboard of entries linked by rumor lines; each location node shows a marker when a *main-progression* entry or link is still missing. Reveals *where* and *which question is open*; withholds what the missing thing is — it won't even say whether you lack a fact or a link ([Steam guide on marker semantics](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=3500382207)). Trigger: passive, player-consulted; the loop reset makes consulting natural ([Edge](https://www.pressreader.com/australia/edge/20180719/282544429072216)). Kelsey Beachum's GDC 2021 rule: *pull, not push* — "make that info available, signal its availability, and let the player decide when to access it"; fellow travellers offer a "where should I go" menu keyed to mood ("I think I'll start with something small" → the moon) ([GDC Vault PDF](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/mediaProxy.php?sid=1027008)). Borrow: a per-location/per-thread "incomplete" marker on the case board that never names the missing item, plus a Watson "where should I look?" tool that answers with a *place*, not a *fact*.

- **Tunic — the manual.** Booklet pages found out of order; diagrams, maps and pencil annotations readable, prose not. Reveals that a secret exists and roughly where ("bury secrets in the manual"); withholds any mechanical unlock — "nothing in the game unlocks mechanically based on having a page. It's just a piece of paper" (Shouldice, [NME](https://www.nme.com/features/tunic-takes-the-retro-manual-rips-it-up-and-turns-it-into-a-meta-game-mechanic-2986386)); "if you frame that communication as a mystery to be solved, then the revelation is all the sweeter" ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/tunic-s-developer-on-creating-the-in-game-manual-full-of-mysteries)). Pages are "meted out when the story or pace of exploration deems them necessary". Borrow: hints as *found documents* (a previous investigator's annotated notebook page) rather than a UI button — help arrives as evidence.

- **Professor Layton — hint coins.** 3–4 hints per puzzle, one coin each, super hint two; coins finite and hidden in scenes by tapping; opened in order, escalating from reframing to near-answer; no score penalty ([Layton wiki](https://layton.fandom.com/wiki/Hints), [StrategyWiki](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Professor_Layton_and_the_Curious_Village/Hint_Coins)). The answer only at the last tier, and the currency is earned by the same looking the game wants. Borrow: if we ever price help, price it in something the player earned by investigating (e.g. spend a "confirmed fact" or an unused observation), never in real time.

- **Ace Attorney (Dual Destinies / Spirit of Justice) — Consult.** Mechanism: after three penalties in one cross-examination a Consult button appears; the partner names the *suspicious statement*, but "you still need to figure out what piece of evidence matches up to it" ([wiki](https://aceattorney.fandom.com/wiki/Trial)); toggleable in the 2024 trilogy. Reveals: *which* line; withholds: *why* and *with what*. Trigger: failure count, in-character. Borrow: exact template for Watson — after N wrong accusations, Watson may say "the timing in Ada's statement bothers me" and nothing more. The player still has to name the contradiction.

- **LucasArts / Monkey Island 2 SE.** Hold a button and Guybrush "offers a clue"; the in-game Hint Line phone (Chester at the LucasArts desk) jokes on the real 1991 phone line; SE hints are progressive ("show another hint") and usage is tracked by achievements for 0 and <10 hints ([MI2 wiki](https://monkeyisland.fandom.com/wiki/Monkey_Island_2:_LeChuck's_Revenge), [PS trophy guide](https://www.playstationtrophies.org/game/monkey-island-2-le-chucks-revenge/guide/)). Everything is available; the price is a badge, not a wall. Borrow: a visible "solved without help" mark per chapter makes asking optional and honest without hiding help.

- **Myst / Riven — no hint system.** Miller: "once you understand the solution…you blame yourself for not finding the answer and not us"; "subtle clue solutions" — "a flash of light on the other side of the world, a small 440 volt sign, a license plate on a desk" — "make the experience more about paying attention than just trying to get into our heads" ([PlayStation Blog Blow × Miller](https://blog.playstation.com/archive/2017/08/24/the-witness-jonathan-blow-talks-game-design-with-myst-co-creator-rand-miller/)). Borrow: the first fix for "stuck with everything in hand" is usually a missing scene cue, not a hint — audit whether the room *itself* points at the contradiction.

- **The Witness — Blow.** No text hints; help is structural — "discrete panels located at obvious places in the world" that "auto-position" you on approach (Blow, same link); clarity over red herrings ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/feature-jonathan-blow-on-designing-i-the-witness-i-)). Withholds everything except *this is a puzzle, here is its front*. Borrow: make "the accusation panel" unmistakable so a stuck player at least knows the shape of the answer required (who / what / when), not just that one exists.

- **Blue Prince — walk away.** Ros: "you always have the option to move on and keep exploring"; "puzzle difficulty is largely determined by the player themselves…whenever they feel they have enough information" ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/blue-prince-puzzle-game-interview/)). No hints; the valve is parallel threads. Borrow: never let one contradiction be the sole gate — always keep ≥2 open leads so "stuck" can be routed around.

- **Baba Is You — none.** Levels designed backwards from a satisfying trick; "difficulty is [not] a crucial part of the game" ([Game Pilgrim](https://gamepilgrim.com/2019/04/06/developer-interview-hempuli-oy/)); the only help is a wide-open level select — breadth as the hint.

- **Zelda companions — the anti-pattern.** Navi/Fi: interrupt, repeat, tell what the player already knows; Miyamoto conceded Navi "would often say the same things over and over" ([Zelda Dungeon](https://www.zeldadungeon.net/hey-listen-a-look-at-the-problems-of-links-companions/)). A *pushed* hint with no memory of what the player already knows is the medium's most hated help design. Borrow: Watson never volunteers; and when asked, Watson must diff against the notebook so it never restates a card already in hand.

- **Hitman — Mission Stories guidance Full / Minimal / Off.** Opportunities exist as accessibility — "you only have 10 minutes to play? Here's an Opportunity" — and were kept as "the 90 percenters", paths seeded with systemic things you notice on the way ([Game Developer Q&A](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/q-a-lessons-learned-from-five-years-spent-on-the-i-hitman-i-series)); Master difficulty forces Off ([wiki](https://hitman.fandom.com/wiki/Mission_Stories)). Reveals the next step of a chosen thread only; opt-in per level. Borrow: a three-position Watson assist setting chosen at chapter start (Full: names the thread and the piece · Minimal: names the thread · Off), so "feels like the answer" becomes the player's choice.

- **Emily Short / IF.** Her puzzle criteria: explorability — "does the puzzle respond well to failed attempts?" — and fairness — "does it…allow for partial successes?" ([XYZZY](https://xyzzyawards.org/?p=386)). Counterfeit Monkey: "if the player tries to do something that might be in-bounds, but isn't, can we easily and consistently articulate why?"; early puzzles interlinked so progress in one helps another; a puzzle added because testers "wandered around…not sure what to work on" ([Making of](https://emshort.blog/2013/01/24/making-of-counterfeit-monkey-puzzles-and-toys/)). Borrow: every wrong accusation gets a *reasoned* rejection ("that card places Ada in the galley, not on deck — it doesn't contradict her"), which is feedback, not a hint.

- **Universal Hint System (1988–).** Question-first, hints revealed one at a time "from subtle nudges to full answers" ([UHS about](https://www.uhs-hints.com/about/)). O'Neill's low-spoiler-hints piece adds: neutral headings ("The baby is crying", not "How do I get the candy"), one nudge at a time, self-contained hints, explicit spoiler tiers ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/how-and-why-to-write-low-spoiler-hints-for-adventure-games-)). Borrow: if a ladder exists, its *rungs are questions the player picks*, and the first rung only restates the open question.

- **Thinky-games practice — Can of Wormholes "Gain Insight".** Each hint is a *smaller puzzle isolating the mechanic*; the player must still transfer the insight ([Thinky Games](https://thinkygames.com/news/can-of-wormholes-the-worm-based-puzzle-game-with-a-unique-hint-system-comes-to-steam-march-24th/)). Golden Idol gates each hint behind a word-matching mini-puzzle and phrases hints as attention cues ("consider the food on the dinner table") ([Adventure Game Hotspot](https://adventuregamehotspot.com/review/119/the-case-of-the-golden-idol)). Borrow: a hint that is itself a two-card mini-deduction (same *shape* of contradiction, different content) teaches the move without spending the case.

- **Partial-correctness feedback — Obra Dinn / Golden Idol.** Obra Dinn confirms only when three fates are fully correct; the option space makes guessing slower than deducing ([Intermittent Mechanism](https://intermittentmechanism.blog/2024/05/21/confirmation-in-the-return-of-obra-dinn/)). Golden Idol's scroll reports "filled incorrectly" vs "two or less are incorrect" ([review](https://adventuregamehotspot.com/review/119/the-case-of-the-golden-idol)). Reveals *distance*, withholds *which*. Borrow: Watson's verdict on a submitted theory uses a coarse meter (cold / warm / one piece off) — the cheapest unstuck signal that spoils nothing.

- **Telemetry-driven nudges — Valve, Naughty Dog.** Valve's rule: fix the level, not the player — sterile walls so interactables read, "gates" that force a pause, glass holes above a fizzler to suggest "shoot up" ([GMTK](https://gmtk.substack.com/p/valves-secret-weapon), [Portal 2 commentary](https://theportalwiki.com/wiki/Portal_2_developer_commentary)); the one in-game nudge is diegetic — idle too long and Wheatley "comes up with the idea" himself. Uncharted: "audible hints are delivered if you take too long", optional d-pad-up swings the camera at the target, and hints "are often quite vague" ([Can I Play That](https://caniplaythat.com/2022/01/28/uncharted-legacy-of-thieves-collection-ps5-accessibility-review-can-i-play-that/)). Trigger: idle timer, camera-pointing rather than text. Borrow: log stuck-time per chapter; past a threshold Watson gets an *in-character* idle line pointing at an *area* — and every firing is a playtest note that the scene cue is too weak.

## Recommended stack for Baker (least spoiling first)
1. Reasoned rejection on every wrong accusation (Short).
2. Coarse warm/cold meter on submitted theories (Obra Dinn / Golden Idol).
3. Case-board "incomplete" markers per thread that name no facts (Outer Wilds).
4. ≥2 open leads at all times so stuck can be routed around (Blue Prince).
5. Watson "where should I look" → returns a place (Outer Wilds travellers menu).
6. Failure-count Consult: Watson names *which* statement, never *why* (Ace Attorney).
7. Idle-timer diegetic line that points the camera at an area (Portal 2 / Uncharted).
8. Hint-as-mini-deduction with fresh content (Can of Wormholes / Golden Idol).
9. Assist level Full/Minimal/Off chosen at chapter start; "no assist" badge (Hitman / MI2 SE).
10. Never: pushed, repeated, memory-less companion hints (Navi/Fi).
