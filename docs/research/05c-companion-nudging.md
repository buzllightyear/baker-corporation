# 05c — Companion nudging: how partners push without solving

**Problem this answers.** A playtester talked to Watson for an hour, held every clue, and never accused — nobody signalled "the case is ready." The designer rejects a hint ladder. Below: how strong companions nudge, what triggers a nudge, how they avoid solving, and the rule each one adds to our Watson's voice.

## The literary baseline: Watson conducts, Holmes concludes

- **Allowed to say:** what he saw, what he doesn't understand, and questions. Holmes: "you are not yourself luminous, but you are a conductor of light… a remarkable power of stimulating it" (*Hound*, https://www.sparknotes.com/lit/hound/quotes/). Watson is "a proxy for the reader, assumed to be as baffled as Watson" (http://www.sandfordborins.com/2009/09/17/sherlock-holmes-and-narrative-point-of-view/).
- **Trigger:** Holmes goes quiet or Watson is confused → Watson asks.
- **Avoids solving:** he narrates and questions; the synthesis is Holmes's, on Holmes's schedule.
- **Our rule:** Watson's default speech act is the *question*, not the statement; a nudge is "what do you make of X?" never "X means Y."

## Disco Elysium — skills as voices that argue and are wrong

- **Allowed:** 24 inner voices "arguing, warning, celebrating, and guiding" contextually; Logic "can build an elegant but incorrect theory," Encyclopedia "can bury you in context," Rhetoric pushes fights better left alone (https://gamers.wiki/en/games/disco-elysium/guides/disco-elysium-skills-guide-all-24-voices-and-attributes). Even passed checks can mislead — "a blatant weaponization of the user interface" (https://www.ogradyfilm.com/post/review-disco-elysium). Kurvitz built the system as his answer to choice-and-consequence (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco_Elysium).
- **Trigger:** contextual thresholds — a voice fires when the situation matches its domain and its level.
- **Avoids solving:** voices disagree with each other (Pain Threshold vs Inland Empire, https://steamcommunity.com/app/632470/discussions/0/4328600953646840584/); the player must choose which to trust.
- **Our rule:** Watson may offer *two competing readings* of a clue and must say which one he'd bet on and why; being confidently wrong sometimes is a feature, not a bug.

## BioShock Infinite — Elizabeth points, tosses, never blocks

- **Allowed:** in downtime she wanders to "look at" markers (paintings, docks) so she reads as a fascinated child; in combat she calls out enemies with a theatre-style spotlight and tosses ammo/health; environment points are tagged "more/less interesting" so she interacts "without getting in the player's way" (Abercrombie GDC 2014, https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1020831/Bringing-BioShock-Infinite-s-Elizabeth ; https://www.escapistmagazine.com/bioshock-infinites-elizabeth-uses-theater-technique-for-ai/ ; talk video https://archive.org/details/GDC2014Abercrombie). Levine calls her "a very you-centered AI" and keeps scenes "very short" so the player stays "active, active, active" (https://gamingbolt.com/bioshock-infinite-elizabeth-is-a-very-you-centered-ai-says-levine ; https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/ken-levine-on-the-storytelling-craft-of-i-bioshock-infinite-i-).
- **Trigger:** player idle → she does something *watchable*; combat → she supplies resources.
- **Avoids solving:** she directs attention (spotlight), never resolves; she gives you ammo, not the kill.
- **Our rule:** when the human idles, Watson should *go do something visible on the ship* (re-read a log, stare at a hatch) rather than talk — attention direction beats explanation.

## Firewatch — Delilah as a voice that mostly reacts

- **Allowed:** she responds to what Henry reports; the game picks "the truest and most specific thing" from ~10,000 events, conditioned on time of day and what was said in the last 20 seconds (https://slate.com/culture/2016/02/the-video-game-firewatch-and-its-origins-in-1980s-text-adventures.html ; https://www.pastemagazine.com/games/firewatch-interview/).
- **Trigger:** player initiates via radio; silence is a legal choice.
- **Avoids solving:** her replies are hand-timed with deliberate delay so she seems to "consider what you've said"; she has opinions, not answers.
- **Our rule:** Watson replies to what the human *reports*, and a short pause before a reply reads as thought — no instant verdicts.

## Portal 2 — Wheatley, the wrong companion

- **Allowed:** informal, improvised-sounding speech; Wolpaw wanted a sidekick who sounds like he's "making things up as you go along" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheatley_(Portal)). He is by design the "dumbest moron who ever lived"; his hacking is "inept or misleading."
- **Trigger:** he narrates constantly; guidance arrives whether asked or not.
- **Avoids solving:** he cannot — his wrongness is the plot.
- **Our rule:** a companion whose confidence exceeds his accuracy is *charming if the player can catch him*; Watson's errors must be catchable from evidence the human already holds.

## Ace Attorney — Maya/Athena and the "Consult" button

- **Allowed:** partners restate stakes and react; Takumi's rule: "I make it so players can identify contradictions on their own" (https://gyakutensaibanlibrary.blogspot.com/2020/12/creator-interview-takumi-shu-2012.html). In Dual Destinies, after three penalties a **Consult** button appears; the partner "point[s] out the false statement, although you still need to figure out what piece of evidence matches" (https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Phoenix_Wright:_Ace_Attorney_-_Dual_Destinies/Gameplay ; https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/VisualNovel/PhoenixWrightAceAttorneyDualDestinies). Some players still felt it gave "too many hints" (https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/939946-phoenix-wright-ace-attorney-dual-destinies/67805740).
- **Trigger:** repeated failure (3 strikes), *player-pressed*.
- **Avoids solving:** narrows *where* to look, never *what* to present.
- **Our rule:** Watson's strongest nudge is "the contradiction is somewhere in *this* testimony," and it only unlocks after a rejected theory and only if asked.

## Frogwares — Watson as recorder, Jon as foil

- **Allowed:** Crimes & Punishments' deduction board lets clues link into 3–5 solutions per case; the player "can fail or succeed in finding the culprit" and the game does not stop you (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes:_Crimes_%26_Punishments). In Chapter One, Jon is an imaginary friend who quips, pranks, and keeps a journal that "praises or scolds Sherlock depending on how skillfully you complete the game's tasks" — he "does not provide hints or solutions"; conclusions get only "subtle (and inconclusive) suggestions" (https://adventuregamehotspot.com/review/266/sherlock-holmes-chapter-one).
- **Trigger:** end of task → journal entry; never mid-deduction.
- **Avoids solving:** Mind Palace offers *interpretations*, the player picks; Jon comments on process, not answers.
- **Our rule:** Watson keeps a visible casebook that grades *how* the human investigated (thoroughness, contradictions ignored), which is itself a readiness meter without being a hint.

## Pentiment — the deadline is the nudge

- **Allowed:** nobody hints. Sawyer: "we're trying to rule people in, not rule them out"; the timer exists "to make the player not look at it from the perspective of 'I definitely think this person did it,' because it's difficult to have that level of confidence" (https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/making-pentiment-s-most-macabre-murder-mysteries). The player always has at least one viable suspect and must present to the Archdeacon regardless (https://www.thegamer.com/josh-sawyer-interview-pentiment-fallout-new-vegas/ ; https://www.shacknews.com/article/146276/pentiment-josh-sawyer-no-correct-answer).
- **Trigger:** calendar, not competence.
- **Avoids solving:** alibis were removed so no suspect is ever cleared; certainty is the thing withheld.
- **Our rule:** a diegetic clock (the ship docks, the captain convenes) is the cleanest "case is ready" signal — readiness is a *world event*, not a Watson opinion.

## Return of the Obra Dinn — the book as silent partner

- **Allowed:** the logbook auto-fills basics per death; correct fates confirm only in sets of three, a rule Pope added "to deter guesswork" (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_of_the_Obra_Dinn ; https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/for-lucas-pope-i-return-of-the-obra-dinn-i-was-a-bunch-of-appealing-design-problems). "If you have three filled in and the game isn't saying anything, you've got something wrong" (https://intermittentmechanism.blog/2024/05/21/confirmation-in-the-return-of-obra-dinn/).
- **Trigger:** structural — the player fills entries; the book responds.
- **Avoids solving:** it says *yes* in batches and never says *which* one was wrong.
- **Our rule:** the page's theory-judge should confirm in *bundles* ("two of these three hold") — coarse, batched truth-feedback tells the human they're close without pointing.

## Zelda — Fi and Navi: the over-hinting backlash

- **Allowed (what went wrong):** Fi repeated "the known" and "ruin[ed] the discovery of the unknown with her prophetic advice" (https://gameranx.com/updates/id/4544/article/skyward-sword-s-fi-is-more-annoying-than-navi/). Aonuma: fans "were really bummed out that they couldn't find the hidden element of the game… getting lost isn't that bad" (https://mynintendonews.com/2016/06/16/aonuma-said-that-changes-in-zelda-breath-of-the-wild-were-made-based-on-negative-skyward-sword-feedback/ ; https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2014/06/eiji_aonuma_vows_to_reduce_hand_holding_in_future_zelda_installments). HD remaster made Fi's advice opt-in.
- **Trigger (bad):** unconditional, repeated.
- **Our rule:** Watson never restates a clue the human has already opened, and never volunteers a hint that hasn't been asked for or earned.

## Uncharted / God of War — the timing lesson

- **Allowed:** Uncharted 4 shows a d-pad prompt "if players are delayed in progressing"; the hint is opt-in per instance and toggleable (https://www.familygamingdatabase.com/accessibility/Uncharted+4+A+Thiefs+End). TLOU2 offers Off/Sometimes/Frequent (https://www.naughtydog.com/blog/the_last_of_us_part_ii_accessibility_features_detailed). GoW Ragnarök's companions blurted solutions; Matt Sophos: "The timing for the first hint was too aggressive and should have had a much longer countdown timer" — and "we never saw feedback about the characters talking too much" in playtests (https://www.playstationlifestyle.net/2023/02/16/god-of-war-ragnarok-puzzles-spoiled-oversight/).
- **Trigger:** idle timer → *offer* a hint (Uncharted) vs idle timer → *deliver* a hint (GoW). The first was fine; the second was the scandal.
- **Our rule:** an idle timer may make Watson *offer* ("want me to think out loud?"), never deliver; and playtests won't catch over-hinting — log it.

## LLM-companion mystery games (2023–2026)

- **Uncover the Smoking Gun (ReLU/Krafton):** suspects are GPT-4o; essential facts tagged `[Confirmed Info]`, scripted lies tagged `[System Overload]`, everything else is free generation; hallucination was deliberately folded into design ("determining when and how false information should appear") (https://premortem.games/2024/07/10/solve-the-case-by-chatting-with-gpt-in-uncover-the-smoking-gun-by-relu-games/). No partner character; the *page* labels what's canon.
- **Vaudeville (Bumblebee):** the cautionary case — NPCs "giving contradictory information and not realising or acknowledging that fact," "trapped in a looping conversation," no sense of completion (https://www.keengamer.com/articles/previews/vaudeville-preview-the-ai-questioning-to-nowhere/).
- **Suck Up! (Proxima):** NPCs have a "suspicion threshold" — a hidden numeric gate the model can't talk its way past (https://wanderfolk.ai/best-ai-npc-games-2026/).
- **Dead Meat (Meaning Machine, 2025):** "Game Conscious AI"; a critic notes ACE "lacks game logic that the model can't override" — logic must sit outside the model (https://www.nvidia.com/en-us/geforce/news/nvidia-ace-autonomous-ai-companions-pubg-naraka-bladepoint/ ; Bristol study forthcoming https://bristoldigitalgamelab.blogs.bristol.ac.uk/2025/05/05/recap-dead-meat-player-study/).
- **Case 429 (2026 research):** "Watson" is the AI whose summaries are biased; the player wins "only by systematically disproving flawed machine conclusions" (https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3744736.3749366).
- **Our rule:** the model never holds the truth (already true for us); canon-vs-generated must be *labelled* to the human (Smoking Gun's tags), and every "I'm sure" from Watson must be paired with the evidence IDs it rests on so it can be checked.

## Synthesis for Baker Corporation

Readiness must come from the **world and the page**, not from Watson's mouth: a diegetic deadline (Pentiment), a casebook that fills and grades process (Jon, Obra Dinn), and batched theory feedback ("two of three hold") together tell the human "you have enough" without saying what. Watson's voice: asks, reports, offers competing readings, is sometimes wrong in checkable ways, never restates opened clues, never names the culprit, and — when the human idles — *offers* to think aloud rather than doing it. Since Watson cannot accuse, his one structural line is allowed: "Holmes, I have nothing left to fetch — the rest is yours."
