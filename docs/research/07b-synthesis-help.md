# Research 7b — Synthesis-stage help: what to offer a player who holds every clue and still cannot name the culprit

Source: research subagent, 2026-09-04. Scope: on-demand help *after* the board is full — the game has confirmed "you hold every load-bearing clue", the player says "I don't get it." 05b covered *where to look*; 05c covered partner tone. This covers what a player can ask for at synthesis, what comes back, and what a Watson that can actually run `cross_check` / `timeline` should be allowed to do.

## The pattern

Systems that survive this stage do one of four things, never the fifth:
1. **Re-index** the material (recaps, comic strips, thought routes) — same facts, different order.
2. **Point at a doubt**, not an answer — *which* statement or *which* slot is wrong (Consult, Mood Matrix, Golden Idol's "two or fewer").
3. **Sell the deduction in priced tiers** with the final step held back (Layton super hint, UHS, Roottrees duck).
4. **Turn the question back on the player** ("do you understand?", "list who could *not*") — elimination as a method, not a result.
5. *(Never)* name the culprit. Even Layton's answer-tier hides the last step.

## Case studies

- **Professor Layton — hint coins.** Ask: "hint" on a puzzle; 3 tiers (4 from *Unwound Future*), 1 coin each, the Super Hint 2 coins. Tiers go restate → narrow → method; the Super Hint "practically gives out the answer, explaining all steps … hiding the last part of it from the player" ([Layton wiki: Hints](https://layton.fandom.com/wiki/Hints), [StrategyWiki](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Professor_Layton_and_the_Unwound_Future/Hint_Coins)). Withholds: the final move; and coins are finite, hidden in scenes, so spending is a decision. Pacing: player-initiated, escalating cost, no gate on attempts. **Watson:** tiered *cost* is the transferable idea — the 4th tier can lay out the whole chain and still stop one inference short of a name.

- **Ace Attorney — Consult / Mood Matrix / Perceive.** Consult unlocks only after 3 penalties or a restart; the partner "points out the false statement, although you still need to figure out what piece of evidence matches up to it" ([RPGSite guide](https://www.rpgsite.net/guide/15373-phoenix-wright-ace-attorney-dual-destinies-walkthrough-a-spoiler-free-guide-to-every-case-episode), [GameFAQs thread on Consult](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/boards/939946-phoenix-wright-ace-attorney-dual-destinies/68061393)). Mood Matrix: the goal is to point out an emotion that contradicts the words, and finding the discord ends the sequence like presenting evidence ends a cross-exam ([Ace Attorney wiki](https://aceattorney.fandom.com/wiki/Mood_Matrix), [Capcom localisation blog](https://news.capcomusa.com/zeroobjections/blog/2013/10/17/phoenix-wright-ace-attorney-dual-destinies-localization-the-trials-and-tribulations-of-the-gameplay-mechanics-team)). Perceive/Mood Matrix are *instruments that surface where to doubt* while leaving the link unnamed. Withholds: the evidence and the inference. Pacing: earned by failure (Consult) or granted as a scene mechanic. **Watson:** "which statement is suspect" is exactly `cross_check`'s output — Watson can say *this testimony fails against the evidence*, and stop.

- **Ace Attorney "Revisualization" / Danganronpa "Closing Argument" & "Logic Dive" — recaps as puzzles.** Revisualization is "a series of multiple choice questions about items and evidence … no penalty for choosing incorrectly … re-examining pieces of evidence from much earlier in the case that don't line up" ([Ace Attorney wiki: Revisualization](https://aceattorney.fandom.com/wiki/Revisualization)). Closing Argument is a manga strip with missing panels the player fills from a pool that includes wrong panels ([Danganronpa wiki: Class Trials](https://danganronpa.fandom.com/wiki/Class_Trials)); Logic Dive is the same as forked multiple-choice on a rail, criticised for asking players to skim text they need ([Gamer Horizon](https://gamerhorizon.com/2014/09/04/danganronpas-minigames-make-great-visual-novel-worse/)). Withholds: nothing new — the recap contains only facts the player has, in canonical order; the help is the *ordering*. Pacing: scripted at the climax. **Watson:** a `timeline` rebuild is our Revisualization — re-order what the player holds, with the gaps left as gaps.

- **Return of the Obra Dinn — no hints; community's elimination method.** No hint feature. Community advice: "the game works on a process of elimination"; guess early and let the 3-at-a-time lock-in check you; use language, role, and the group photo; "identify the other topmen first"; "there's only one person this man could be" once the others are placed ([GameFAQs Basic Hints](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/switch/272725-return-of-the-obra-dinn/faqs/79976/basic-hints), [Nudge guide](https://gameplay.tips/guides/3243-return-of-the-obra-dinn.html), [Steam "Last Few Poor Souls"](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1574915095)). Withholds: everything except correctness-in-threes. Post-hoc: leaving early gets "a letter chastising you"; the last two fates open only after full completion ([GameFAQs: The End](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/pc/248186-return-of-the-obra-dinn/faqs/80340/x-the-end)). **Watson:** the unstick move players invent on their own is *negative* — who this could NOT be — and the map/timeline (where each body was, in which chapter) is the tool they draw it with.

- **The Case of the Golden Idol — gated hints + "two or fewer".** Hint button first demands a word-to-object matching test "to discourage overuse", then offers four general hints "that suggest a way to think about the characters and their motivation rather than spelling out the answer" ([Nintendo World Report](http://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/63926/the-case-of-the-golden-idol-switch-review), [Intermittent Mechanism](https://intermittentmechanism.blog/2024/05/06/the-case-of-the-case-of-the-golden-idol-the-golden-idol-of-puzzle-video-games-by-sallie-hinkle/)). The scroll reports green / "two or fewer incorrect" / red; Klavins: full validation "would eliminate deduction; no feedback creates hopelessness", the aim is "steady progress and getting stuck until an insight strikes" ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/case-of-the-golden-idol)). Withholds: *which* slots are wrong. **Watson:** grade the player's *draft theory* as distance ("one link off") without naming the link — our `submit_theory` already returns a grade; Watson can translate it into "close, one hinge wrong".

- **The Roottrees Are Dead — rubber duck + Evidence Intuition + lock-in.** Duck: "a first hint gives you a more global hint. Asking for a second will give a bit more direct guidance, like what search term to use"; third tier says it outright ([Can I Play That](https://caniplaythat.com/2025/12/29/indie-spotlight-the-roottrees-are-dead/), [Adventure Game Hotspot](https://adventuregamehotspot.com/review/3671/the-roottrees-are-dead)). Evidence Intuition tags each evidence item with the count of unresolved clues it still holds — a diff between *what you own* and *what you have locked*; profiles lock in batches so mistakes surface indirectly. Withholds: the inference; only points at the *document that still has unused weight*. Pacing: any time, free, no penalty. **Watson:** the diff is the mechanism — "of your 14 clues, these three have not been used by any theory you've submitted" is computable and not an answer.

- **Her Story — "do you think you understand?"** After enough key clips a chat from "SB" asks whether you understand why she did it; answering ends the game — the prompt hands *completion* to the player's judgement rather than a grader ([Intermittent Mechanism analysis](https://intermittentmechanism.blog/2020/06/13/we-had-a-list-of-rules-an-analysis-of-her-story/); Barlow "has no intention of weighing in" on the ending — [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/the-truth-about-her-story-should-remain-a-mystery-/1100-6429086/)). Withholds: everything; it never says what "understanding" is. **Watson:** ask the question back — "tell me your story of the night in three sentences" — and let the gaps in the telling be the help.

- **Pentiment / Disco Elysium — voices that disagree.** Sawyer: "there cannot be a right answer… some people seem more likely to have done it in retrospect, but that doesn't mean they did it"; the game never tells you you were wrong ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/146276/pentiment-josh-sawyer-no-correct-answer)). Disco's 24 skills "offer you their own advice, and argue among themselves" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/your-skills-talk-to-you-in-disco-elysium-an-inventive-rpg-that-keeps-impressing/)). Withholds: consensus. **Watson:** when asked "who do you think?", answer with *two competing readings* of the same evidence and what would separate them — disagreement as help.

- **Paradise Killer — post-hoc trial.** The trial's reach "depends on how diligently you unlocked Suspect and Crime Case Files"; the game "doesn't judge or ask the player to retry" and "if you did your job… no new information comes to light" ([Neoseeker trials](https://www.neoseeker.com/paradise-killer/walkthrough/Trials), [Jump Dash Roll](https://www.jumpdashroll.com/article/paradise-killer-review)). Lesson: the "what you missed" reveal belongs *after* the second accusation, as a debrief, not a hint.

- **LLM detective games 2024–2026.** *Uncover the Smoking Gun*: suspects hit "system overload" on sensitive questions (lying signal), repeated overload flips them into Confession mode; conclusions are a short-answer sheet, re-submittable, graded indirectly via news articles; partner Qbot gives "continuous help with deduction" ([NamuWiki](https://en.namu.wiki/w/Uncover%20the%20Smoking%20Gun), [Gamecritics](https://gamecritics.com/david-bakker/uncover-the-smoking-gun-review/)). *DetectiveGPT*: "authored case files with fixed culprits… the AI can make the conversation feel more natural, but the underlying truth is supposed to stay locked" ([devlog](https://kkkkkom.itch.io/detectivegpt/devlog/1587729/detectivegpt-is-live-can-a-mystery-game-work-through-natural-language-chat)). *Doki Doki AI Interrogation*: an emotion circuit raises heartbeat until the suspect wants to confess ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2844700/Doki_Doki_AI_Interrogation/)). *Vaudeville*: free dialogue, 48% positive — the LLM adds texture, not deduction ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/how-does-vaudeville-work-ai-murder-mystery-game-explained-2216792/)). None lets the AI *partner* name the culprit; the ones that work keep the truth in an authored file the model cannot see, and make the model's tells (overload, heartbeat) *pointers*, not verdicts. **Watson:** same architecture — the page holds the truth and grades; Watson sees only what the player unlocked.

- **Universal Hint System.** Since 1988: nested questions, each opened one line at a time, "starting with very general advice and progressively becoming more specific until the solution is revealed" ([UHS about](https://www.uhs-hints.com/about/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Hint_System)). The unit is the *question the player picks*, so the reveal is scoped to what they asked. **Watson:** offer a menu of questions ("why doesn't the 9:40 alibi hold?"), not a hint button.

- **Elimination tools — Cluedo sheet, logic grids, Obra Dinn map.** Cluedo "can be a logic puzzle akin to Sudoku" with advanced notation and cascades ([Steam guide](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=1388570978)); the grid rule is "the moment you mark a match, every other cell in that row and column must be an elimination" ([Puzzle Labs](https://www.thepuzzlelabs.com/grid-puzzles/rules), [Brilliant](https://brilliant.org/wiki/elimination-grids/)). What the tool provides is a *structure with empty cells*, not filled cells.

## What Watson should offer when asked "I don't get it"

Watson has real tools; the danger is that a real `cross_check` over everything *is* the answer. Bound it by three rules.

1. **Ladder, priced by scope, not by explicitness.** Tier 1 (free): rebuild the `timeline` from what the player holds and read it back with gaps as gaps — Revisualization. Tier 2 (free): Roottrees diff — "these clues appear in no theory you've submitted." Tier 3 (one accusation-adjacent cost, e.g. a case-board note stays on record): run `cross_check` on **one statement the player names** — Consult. Tier 4 (Layton super hint, once per case): full chain laid out, last inference withheld.
2. **Elimination pass: allowed, but player-driven and partial.** Watson may say "X could not have done it — here is the timeline conflict", **only for suspects the player puts on the table**, and never for all-but-one. Rule: an elimination pass must leave ≥2 suspects standing, and Watson names the *constraint* (the 9:40 gap) rather than the *survivor*. This mirrors Obra Dinn's community method and the logic-grid rule — the cascade is the player's to run.
3. **When asked "who do you think did it?"** Watson never names; it gives two competing readings of the same evidence (Disco/Pentiment) plus the single check that would separate them, or turns the question back (Her Story): "tell me the night in three sentences." The page's grader stays the only judge, and "what you missed" is a debrief unlocked *after* the second accusation (Paradise Killer, Obra Dinn's letter).
